## 5. Possible retrieval risks

- **Clinical-diagnosis language bleed-through.** Some peer-reviewed student-mental-health papers describe clinical cut-offs (e.g., PHQ-9 ≥ 10) or refer to 'patients'. The RAG prompt and safety-agent must rewrite retrieved sentences into non-diagnostic form (e.g., 'elevated screening score' rather than 'has depression').
- **Healthy Minds Network PDFs were not directly downloadable** from the project environment (HTTP 403 to non-browser User-Agents). DOC_A001–A003 are still included as citation-only records because they are the authoritative source for the Healthy Minds Study instrument. The RAG pipeline should fetch these PDFs from a workstation browser and place them under `pdfs/student_mental_health/` before going live.
- **Paywalled publishers (Springer, Elsevier, Nature, T&F).** Several accepted records point only to a landing page (PMC ID not available, no open-access version). Their abstract snippets should never be quoted as full-text evidence in generated narratives. Treat them as 'background citations only'.
- **XAI / SHAP papers are general methodology**, not mental-health-specific. The system must add a domain-specific interpretation layer so SHAP feature attributions are presented as 'contributions to the model's prediction' — never as causal risk factors for depression or anxiety.
- **RAG / LLM evaluation papers are mostly preprints.** Pin specific arXiv versions (e.g., arXiv:2005.11401 v1, 28 May 2020) so downstream citations remain reproducible.
- **Snippets vs. full text.** For ~11 of 52 records the metadata `main_topic` and `relevance_to_project` fields are derived from search snippets rather than the full PDF. Before going live, verify these against the downloaded PDF text.
- **Duplicate-content risk.** Some PMC and arXiv preprint versions of the same paper may both appear in retrieval. SHA-1 deduplication at index-time is recommended.

## 6. Recommendations for improving the corpus

- **Fetch Healthy Minds Study PDFs manually** (national reports, codebook, standard questionnaire) and place them under `pdfs/student_mental_health/`. These are the single most important dataset-context documents for the project.
- **Add 2–3 papers on calibrated risk communication in non-clinical settings** — e.g., weather-probability communication, screening-test result framing, uncertainty communication — to anchor the non-diagnostic language conventions.
- **Add 1–2 clinician-evaluator human-evaluation studies** of AI-generated medical text. HELM and the hallucination surveys cover most rubric dimensions but a clinician-rubric study would strengthen G × I.
- **Run a duplicate-content sweep at chunk level** during indexing, not only at file level. Two papers from the same authors can share large passages.
- **Profile retrieval after the first RAG experiments** and prune any `risk_of_noise = medium` documents that reduce precision. The current 'maybe' list (148 documents) is the natural reservoir for replacement candidates.
- **Pin arXiv versions** for all category H and I papers so future citations are reproducible.
- **Add a domain-specific calibration paper**: a study that calibrates a depression/anxiety screening model and reports calibration metrics (Brier, ECE) would directly bridge categories H and A/B.

## 7. Safety alignment checklist

- [x] All 52 accepted documents are peer-reviewed, official reports, established methodology preprints (arXiv / NeurIPS), or institutional repository theses/reports.
- [x] No clinical treatment guidelines, medication guides, therapy manuals, or crisis-intervention manuals were included.
- [x] Exclusion criteria applied: documents focused mainly on clinical diagnosis, treatment, therapy, medication, or crisis intervention were rejected (see `metadata/rejected_documents.csv`).
- [x] The corpus supports non-diagnostic screening language: `elevated screening risk`, `model-based estimate`, `associated with`, `XAI factors contributed to the model prediction`, `may benefit from speaking with a qualified counselor or mental-health professional`.
- [x] No document in the corpus supports language such as `the student has depression`, `this factor caused depression`, `the recommended treatment is`, or `the student should take medication`.
- [x] Generated narratives must still pass through a safety-agent that rewrites any retrieved clinical phrasing into non-diagnostic wording before delivery to the end user.
- [x) Healthy Minds Network reports are dataset-context documents, not screening instruments for end-user diagnosis; they document the underlying survey used by the project.
